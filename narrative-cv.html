Short narrative CV

I am a robotics researcher developing intelligent machines for healthcare, rehabilitation, and manufacturing—with 9 journal articles, 4 conference papers, and research across 6 countries. My work addresses settings where the body or handled material is soft, deformable, and easily disturbed.

I lead CASREx, a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Horizon Europe fellowship at Imperial College London (EUR 276,187.92; 24 months) on a cable-driven soft exosuit for upper-arm muscle-impairment rehabilitation. Everyday stability—carrying a cup without spilling when bumped—depends on how the brain adjusts arm stiffness. After stroke, this is often impaired. CASREx combines human experiments, muscle-signal recordings, a digital twin, and machine learning to translate healthy stiffness strategies into adaptive patient assistance.

My career has moved deliberately: Ph.D. at Auckland (RoSE soft esophagus; NZD 39,000 industry funding); postdoc at CUHK (cable-robot modelling; MMT 2025, IEEE TCST 2023); Senior Research Engineer at TransGP (led fabric-robotics programme; IEEE RA-L 2025, T-ASE 2026 accepted, IROS 2026 accepted). I supervise 2 MEng students at Imperial, mentor doctoral researchers at HKU, review for major robotics venues, and was selected for the European Talent Academy 2026.

My aim is a long-term programme on human-centred soft robotics—helping people recover stability, and building automation that works with the real world.

Long narrative CV

Research identity. My contribution sits at the crossover of soft and cable-driven robotics, human-centred control, and learning-enabled systems. Across 9 journal articles, 4 conference papers, and 1 Springer book chapter, I model deformable systems, estimate state under uncertainty, and close the loop with adaptive control.

Current fellowship. I lead CASREx at Imperial—a EUR 276,187.92 MSCA project on a soft cable-driven exosuit for upper-arm rehabilitation. The project targets arm stability during disturbance, not only movement—a gap highlighted by the UK RATULS trial (n=770). Methods include human experiments, EMG, digital twins, and machine learning.

Career arc. Ph.D. (Auckland): RoSE platform, BvW industry contract (NZD 39,000), Soft Robotics and IEEE TIE outputs. Postdoc (CUHK): MMT 2025 and IEEE TCST 2023; 2 ITF grant co-authorships. TransGP (2024–2025): led garment-robotics team; RA-L, T-ASE, IROS outputs. Imperial (2025–): independent fellow, Assistant Supervisor, ETA 2026, Imperial Connect Fund collaborations.

Impact and community. Translation in medical-device testing, garment manufacturing, and rehabilitation. Collaboration across 6 countries. Reviewer for IEEE RA-L, ICRA, ASME JMR. 10+ invited talks. Supervision of 6+ students.

Future. Near-term: validate CASREx as impedance-aware rehabilitation. Medium-term: research programme on human-centred soft and cable-driven robots.
