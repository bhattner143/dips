About

Independent robotics research across soft machines and deformable objects

I develop model-based and learning-enabled robotic systems for healthcare and manufacturing. My work joins soft robotics, cable-driven actuation, deformable-object perception, and control — from RoSE, an early soft-robotic esophagus for endoprosthetic stent testing (Soft Robotics, 2021; later cited in Nature Communications, 2026), through cable-wrapping kinematics for CDPRs, to fabric manipulation for garment production.

As Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow at Imperial College London, I lead CASREx on cable-driven soft exosuits for upper-arm rehabilitation.

Research

Three threads, one systems view

Across healthcare and garment automation, the same questions recur: how to sense soft bodies under occlusion, actuate them safely, and close the loop with models that still work on hardware.

Soft robotics & rehabilitation

RoSE and related platforms for stent testing; CASREx cable-driven exosuits for upper-arm muscle impairment.

Cable-driven systems

Wrapping kinematics and tri-space operational control for complex-geometry CDPRs (MMT 2025; IEEE TCST 2023).

Fabric perception & policy

GLW-ICP alignment, RTFF dual-arm flattening, and sewing automation with mesh-aware imitation learning.

Featured project

GLW-ICP — robotic fabric alignment for sewing

Millimetre-level edge and sewing-line alignment under occlusion using Global–Local Weighted ICP, validated on collar, rectangle, and shirt panels.
